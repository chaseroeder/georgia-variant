> **How we reported this.** This profile comes from press releases, trade-press coverage, and a July 2026
> podcast interview with Onu Okebie. Sources are linked at the bottom.

## The deal

In 2021, Onu Okebie and Brian Boland bought Heritage Trucking, a Charlotte, North Carolina, freight broker that had
specialized in flatbed loads for about four decades, from its original management. They renamed it HTL Freight,
now just HTL. Okebie runs it as CEO; Boland is CFO.

HTL is a *non-asset* logistics company, which means it doesn't own the trucks. A freight broker (also called a
third-party logistics provider, or 3PL) finds a carrier for a shipper's load and keeps the difference between what
the shipper pays and what the carrier charges. The model takes little capital, which is part of why small brokers
are attractive to buyers.

## Getting there

Okebie told the *Supply Chain Now* podcast that he's a CPA by training and started in accounting at Home Depot.
On the side, he bought a single truck, grew that business to about five trucks, and sold it. He then moved into
corporate finance at Vanguard Truck Centers, a truck dealer that was bought by a private equity firm, and that's
where he got hooked on M&A. He went to business school to learn deal-making, then teamed up with a classmate to buy
logistics companies that don't own trucks. Heritage was the first.

## Buy, then build

Since the 2021 buyout, HTL has made five acquisitions. The four that made headlines each added something HTL didn't
have:

- **Matchmaker Logistics** (Wilmington, N.C., November 2021): dry van and drayage, beyond HTL's flatbed roots.
  Okebie said the combined company was "bigger but most importantly better for our customers."
- **ATTS Logistics** (Lake Zurich, Ill., 2022): less-than-truckload (LTL) shipping and a base in the Chicago market.
- **CTS Logistics** (Windham, N.H., 2024): managed transportation, where HTL runs a shipper's whole freight program
  (sometimes called 4PL). Its biggest deal at the time.
- **TS3 Logistics** (California, 2025): refrigerated freight. FreightWaves reported it was the second-largest deal
  and brought HTL to about 70 employees.

Several of the sellers were family businesses that had been run for decades, which is a common source of deals in
the lower middle market: owners want to retire and want a buyer who will keep their people and customers.

The results: in October 2024 the company said revenue was up more than 550% since the buyout, and by mid-2026
Okebie put the growth at about 15 times. That same fall it added two board members with deal experience: Scott
Riddle, longtime CEO of M33 Integrated Solutions, and Michael O'Driscoll, former CFO of the Americas division of
building-materials company CRH.

## What's next

In August 2026, HTL hired Jeff Thomas, a veteran of Uber Freight and M33, as chief operating officer. Boland framed
it as a shift from buying growth to building it: acquisitions laid the base, and "now it's time to build on that
foundation," with organic growth doing more of the work.

## Why it matters if you're a student

This is a path you don't hear about at recruiting events: buy an existing small business and grow it, instead of
starting one from scratch or joining a fund. It rewards the same skills banking and private equity do (accounting,
valuation, financing a deal), but you end up running the company. A few things stand out in HTL's story:

- **Boring industries can be great businesses.** Freight brokerage is unglamorous, fragmented, and full of owners
  looking for a successor.
- **The finance background mattered.** Okebie's CPA training and deal experience came before the CEO title.
- **Buy-and-build is a strategy, not a single deal.** Each acquisition added a type of freight or a region, so HTL
  could sell more services to the customers it already had.
